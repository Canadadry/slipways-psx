A second fold and colony, the slipway drag in free mode with everything
it draws (partial line and cost, snap on a known planet, a crossed
planet, too far, an already linked planet), a slipway built from an
off-screen starting planet, travel along it in network mode with X, a
cancelled drag, and square selecting the colony under the centre from
plain free mode. It is the regression guard for the drag the pad once
lacked (a blind press-release gesture drew nothing while aiming). 401
frames.

f0-98 are pad_colonise's frames verbatim (f0-68 being pad_probe's), so
the galaxy and pad_probe.txt's planet list apply, and p14 (type o) is
colony A. In free mode the cursor is the screen centre (80, 60), marked
by the colour-7 crosshair (x78-82 on y=60, y58-62 on x=80, the centre
pixel undrawn). While dragging, the centre pixel shows the slipway's
end. The dragged line is the slipway's core, two pixels thick: colour 1
once faded in, colour 2 when refused (a target set, slipway_display.c).
Labels are colour 8 (targeting_tooltip_show, probe.c). Each drag state
is read on the first frame after its pan: the camera has no velocity in
pad mode, so it stops there. Readings below come from the framebuffer of
each frame (the capture, or the dump and `make state` of the file cut
after that frame).

The galaxies differ: the galaxy depends on the frame the game starts,
keyboard_slipway clicks the title at f1 and a pad title needs the rows
first (X at f12). The pairing compares the shape, not planet ids: two
colonies and one slipway between them. Both second colonies are type f,
charged $8 and three months by the menu's colonise row; both slipways
cost a route price and one month ($14 here for 72 px, $4 there for
32 px). This replay needs a second fold ($3, one month), since its two
colonies are 71 px apart vertically so that the starting planet is off
screen when X is pressed.
